# 4.1 How Fast Did the World Grow?
**Intelligent Futures · Free Trial Lesson** · From the course *Data Science in Algebra 1*

**Grade band:** Ages 13-16 · Algebra 1 or equivalent  
**Time:** One or two 50-minute periods  
**Standards:** CCSS HSF-LE.A.1, HSF-LE.A.2, HSS-ID.B.6

## Why students need the math
Students want to know when the world will reach 10 billion people. The gaps between milestones shrink and then level off, so they need to test linear and exponential models.

## Learning objectives
- Compare linear and exponential models using world population milestones, 1804-2022
- Use growth rates and gaps between milestones as evidence

## Data
- World population milestones from the United Nations: 1 billion (1804) to 8 billion (2022), included in the lesson
- Optional: your own country's census from its national statistics office

## Free tools
- Google Sheets
- CODAP
- Desmos

## Lesson flow
1. Plot year vs population in billions for the eight UN milestones.
2. Compute the years between milestones: 123, 33, 14, 13, 12, 12, 11.
3. Compute the yearly growth rate between milestones: it rises to about 2% (1960-1974), then falls to about 1.2%.
4. Fit a line to 1960-2022 (about 81 million people a year) and predict 2050 (about 10.2 billion).
5. Compare with the UN projection of about 9.7 billion for 2050 and discuss why growth is slowing: health, education, family size.

## Student handout
1. Graph world population (billions) against year. Describe the shape.
2. Compute the years between each milestone. What pattern do you see?
3. Compute the yearly growth rate between milestones. When was it highest?
4. Fit a line to 1960-2022. Interpret the slope in people per year.
5. Use your line to predict 2050. Compare with the UN projection of about 9.7 billion.
6. Explain why a model that fits the past may not fit the future.

## Exit ticket
Explain with numbers why world growth was close to exponential before 1970 but close to linear since 1990.

## Teacher notes & answer key
- Before 1960 the curve bends upward; from 1960 it is close to a straight line.
- Years between milestones: 123, 33, 14, 13, 12, 12, 11.
- Yearly growth rate: about 0.6% (1804-1927), 1.2% (1927-1960), peak about 2.1% (1960-1974), then falling to about 1.2% (2011-2022).
- Line for 1960-2022: about 0.081 billion (81 million) people per year.
- Line predicts about 10.2 billion in 2050; the UN projects about 9.7 billion because growth rates keep falling.
- Discuss: longer lives, falling child mortality, education and smaller families. Extension: repeat with your own country's census data.
