# Is Our Classroom Breathing? CO₂ Data (Grades 6-12)
**Intelligent Futures · Free Trial Lesson** · From the course *Data Explorers K-12*

**Grade band:** Grades K-12 · Science, Social Studies, ELA, Health/PE, CTE, Elementary  
**Time:** Data collection over one class/day + one 50-minute analysis period  
**Standards:** NGSS MS-ESS3-5, HS-ESS3-6 · VA DS

## Why students need the math
Students want to know when the room gets 'stuffy' and whether opening the door helps; the Keeling curve then shows a rising trend plus a yearly wave (linear + periodic).

## Learning objectives
- Collect classroom CO₂ data and relate it to occupancy
- Compare local data to the Mauna Loa (Keeling) record

## Data
- Classroom CO₂ (ppm) from a databot or any CO₂ sensor over one school day
- NOAA Mauna Loa monthly CO₂ (gml.noaa.gov)

## Free tools
- databot or CO₂ sensor
- CODAP
- NOAA Global Monitoring Lab

## Lesson flow
1. Log CO₂ every minute for a class period, noting when students enter, leave and when doors open.
2. Plot time vs ppm; annotate events.
3. Download NOAA monthly data; plot 1958-today.
4. Fit a line to the long-term trend; describe the seasonal wave.
5. Write a recommendation for ventilation at your school.

## Student handout
1. Predict: what will happen to CO₂ when the room fills with students?
2. Mark on your graph when students entered, left, and when doors/windows opened.
3. What was the highest CO₂ reading? When did it happen?
4. Open the NOAA Mauna Loa monthly data. Describe the two patterns you see.
5. Fit a line to the long-term trend. What is the slope in ppm per year?
6. Why does the Mauna Loa record go up and down every year?
7. Write a recommendation for ventilation at our school using one number from your data.

## Exit ticket
Make a claim about your classroom using one number from your graph.

## Teacher notes & answer key
- Indoor CO₂ commonly climbs from outdoor levels (~420+ ppm) to well over 1,000 ppm in a closed, full classroom; opening doors produces a visible drop.
- Mauna Loa shows a rising trend plus a yearly cycle: Northern Hemisphere plants take up CO₂ in spring/summer and release it in fall/winter.
- Recent decades rise roughly 2-2.5 ppm per year; have students compute their own slope from the NOAA file rather than quoting this.
- Sensor options: databot, Vernier, PocketLab, or any classroom CO₂ monitor that exports CSV.

## Extensions
- Algebra 2: model the seasonal wave with a sine function on top of the line.
- Compare classroom data across periods with different class sizes.
