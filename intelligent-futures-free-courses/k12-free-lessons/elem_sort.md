# Teach the Computer to Sort (Grades 2-5)
**Intelligent Futures · Free Trial Lesson** · From the course *Data Explorers K-12*

**Grade band:** Grades K-12 · Science, Social Studies, ELA, Health/PE, CTE, Elementary  
**Time:** Two 30-40 minute sessions  
**Standards:** AI4K12 Big Idea 3 · CCSS 2.MD.D.10

## Why students need the math
Children see that a computer only 'knows' what it was shown.

## Learning objectives
- Sort objects by attributes and make a picture graph
- Train a computer to sort pictures and test it

## Data
- Classroom objects or picture cards (leaves, blocks, recyclables)

## Free tools
- Paper cards
- Teachable Machine (teacher-led)
- CODAP or chart paper

## Lesson flow
1. Students sort 20 cards by a rule they choose; classmates guess the rule.
2. Make a picture graph of how many cards are in each group.
3. Teacher trains Teachable Machine with student-held objects (2 groups).
4. Test with a tricky object; students explain the mistake.
5. Add more examples together and test again.

## Student handout
1. How did you sort your cards? What was your rule?
2. Make a picture graph: how many cards are in each group?
3. What did the computer get right? What did it get wrong?
4. Why do you think the computer made that mistake?
5. What did we add to help the computer learn?

## Exit ticket
Draw the computer's mistake and tell how you fixed it.

## Teacher notes & answer key
- Look for attribute language: color, size, shape, living/non-living.
- Typical mistake: an object the computer never saw, or one held in front of a different background.
- Big idea for kids: computers learn from examples we give them; more and different examples help.

## Extensions
- Grades 4-5: count right and wrong guesses and make a bar graph of accuracy.
- Try Code.org AI for Oceans.
