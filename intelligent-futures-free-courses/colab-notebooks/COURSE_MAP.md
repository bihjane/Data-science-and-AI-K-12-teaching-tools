## Project briefs

### Project: Model a Trend From Your Country

Students choose a trend from their own country, such as population, phone subscriptions or school enrolment, and build the best model they can in Colab.

**Students hand in:**
- Colab notebook with code and comments
- One graph with the data and the chosen model
- A one-page explanation of the model and its limits

**Steps:**
1. Pick a question and download data from the World Bank, Our World in Data or a national statistics office.
2. Copy the Lab notebook and replace the data.
3. Fit at least two models and compare their errors.
4. Predict 5 and 25 years ahead and say which prediction to trust more.
5. Present the result in 3 minutes.

**Success criteria:**
- Question and data source are clear
- Two models compared with an error measure
- Prediction uses correct units
- Limits of the model are explained

Starter notebook: [Open in Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/bihjane/data-science-and-ai-k-12-teaching-tools/blob/main/intelligent-futures-free-courses/colab-notebooks/02_world_population_linear_vs_exponential.ipynb)

### Project: Build, Test and Explain a Classifier

Students apply the full classification workflow to a new dataset and explain the model's math, accuracy and fairness.

**Students hand in:**
- Colab notebook
- Confusion matrix and cross-validation results
- Model card

**Steps:**
1. Choose a dataset: wine, breast cancer (scikit-learn) or an approved Kaggle set.
2. Follow the Lab steps: explore, split, pipeline, train, evaluate.
3. Tune one setting and report the change.
4. Check accuracy for two subgroups if the data allows.
5. Write a model card: purpose, data, accuracy, limits and possible harms.

**Success criteria:**
- Correct train/test workflow
- Evaluation goes beyond accuracy
- Math of the model explained in plain words
- Model card names real limits and risks

Starter notebook: [Open in Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/bihjane/data-science-and-ai-k-12-teaching-tools/blob/main/intelligent-futures-free-courses/colab-notebooks/04_classification_iris_full_pipeline.ipynb)

### Capstone Project: An AI Model for a Real Stakeholder

Teams build a model that answers a real question for someone in their community, compare models, and present a model card.

**Students hand in:**
- Colab notebook
- Comparison of at least three models against a baseline
- Model card and a 5-minute presentation to the stakeholder

**Steps:**
1. Interview a stakeholder and write the question.
2. Find or collect data and describe it.
3. Start from a Lab notebook that fits the task (regression, classification or clustering).
4. Train a baseline and at least two models; test on unseen data.
5. Explain the best model and its mistakes.
6. Write the model card and present.

**Success criteria:**
- Stakeholder question drives the work
- Fair test on unseen data
- Errors explained with examples
- Responsible AI: bias, privacy and misuse addressed

Starter notebook: [Open in Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/bihjane/data-science-and-ai-k-12-teaching-tools/blob/main/intelligent-futures-free-courses/colab-notebooks/03_life_expectancy_regression_gapminder.ipynb)

### Project: Adapt a Notebook for Your Class

Teachers adapt one Lab notebook to their subject, students and local data, then teach it.

**Students hand in:**
- Adapted Colab notebook
- Lesson plan with timing
- Reflection after teaching

**Steps:**
1. Run all six Lab notebooks as a learner.
2. Choose one and replace the data with a local or subject dataset.
3. Add Predict prompts before each code cell (PRIMM).
4. Teach it and collect two samples of student work.
5. Share the notebook with the cohort.

**Success criteria:**
- Notebook runs from start to finish
- Data fits the subject and students
- Prompts make students predict and explain
- Reflection uses student evidence

Starter notebook: [Open in Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/bihjane/data-science-and-ai-k-12-teaching-tools/blob/main/intelligent-futures-free-courses/colab-notebooks/01_first_model_linear_regression.ipynb)
